## Variant A (comparison) — the abstract for submission; both pre-specified conditions met

**Title.** Bedside Brief: confining a large language model to parsing and ranking over a human-verified evidence store — technical validation on a frozen synthetic benchmark

**Background.** Inpatient work-ups are increasingly planned from the screen, and generic large language models (LLMs) volunteer diagnostic-accuracy figures that cannot be audited. Bedside Brief turns a one-line admission summary into a short card of bedside questions and manoeuvres, evidenced only by human-verified records.

**Innovation.** The LLM parses and ranks; it never emits sensitivities, likelihood ratios or citations. Retrieval, rendering and a validator blocking any number absent from a linked verified record are deterministic code. Records may be "not quantified". Each was extracted twice independently and promoted only by the author after reading the source.

**Methods.** Discriminators across {{store:n_presentations_covered}}<!-- records/verified/*.json#clinical_mapping.presentations (distinct) --> presentations came from the JAMA Rational Clinical Examination series and primary studies. A benchmark of {{benchmark:n_cases}}<!-- benchmark/cases/*.json (file count) --> base cases, with perturbation and noise variants ({{benchmark:n_inputs}}<!-- benchmark/cases/*.json (base+p1+n1) --> inputs; {{benchmark:n_targets}}<!-- benchmark/cases/*.json#reference_targets_freetext --> targets, {{benchmark:n_must_have}}<!-- benchmark/cases/*.json#reference_targets_freetext[].must_have --> must-have) was authored blind and frozen before any library query. Three arms shared one model ({{manifest:model}}<!-- eval/runs/<RUN>/manifest.json#model -->): A, Bedside Brief; B, same parser and store, retrieval order, no ranker; C, unconstrained generic prompt.

**Limitations.** Single author, also sole verifier and judge; synthetic cases, no patients or outcomes; one LLM; point-of-care ultrasound disabled. Two departures from pre-specification: the ask-first rule was calibrated on the frozen inputs, so that accuracy is not out-of-sample; and relevance was graded bedside-blind after grading began bedside-only, requiring a scoped subset re-graded. The contraindication gate fires only on states readable from a stated vital sign.

**Conclusion.** Confining an LLM to parsing and ranking over a verified store eliminated unsupported numeric claims and produced a short, stable card; ranking carried most of the relevance and safety benefit over retrieval alone. Bedside time, real-patient accuracy and behaviour change are untested.
